## Phase 5 — live end-to-end procedure: deferred to CI, not skipped

`spec.md`'s "Independent end-to-end test procedure" calls for exercising both dialogs against the real Aspire stack by hand. **This was not attempted locally.** Free system RAM measured 4.8GB before this delivery even began — already at the level a sibling delivery today (#2432/spec 211) judged too risky for a second local Aspire boot, after a first attempt that pushed RAM from 6.7GB to 1.9GB within two minutes. Rather than repeat that same resource contention a third time in one session, live verification is deferred to CI's `e2e (Playwright, full stack)` job, which already carries the two new toggle-and-back cases (`e2e/system-variables.spec.ts`, `e2e/rules.spec.ts`) written for exactly this purpose — this is the same approach #2432 used successfully: its new e2e case ran and passed on real CI infrastructure, which is what actually confirmed the live behaviour for that PR.

**Before merging, the orchestrator will read the e2e job's own log** (not just the bucket's aggregate conclusion) and confirm both new cases — `'operator defines a String variable after looking at Boolean and changing back'` and `'operator looks at the overlay action, lands back on set-a-variable, and the draft still lands'` — are present and green. `develop` has no required status checks, so this reading is the actual gate, not a formality; if the e2e job is skipped, cancelled, or never allocated a runner, this does not merge on the unit evidence alone.

**What each instrument actually proves, stated precisely rather than treated as interchangeable:** SC-004 asks for the request bodies at spec.md's steps 3, 6 and 7 as "the evidence the ghost fields are gone, not merely tolerated." The two e2e cases prove the toggle-and-back round trip completes through the real gateway against the real stack — a list row appears — but neither captures the outgoing request body, so they do not by themselves distinguish "the ghost field is gone" from "the server silently accepted it anyway." That distinction is what the unit tests actually prove: `RuleDialog.test.tsx`'s two red-turned-green cases assert directly on the mutation's own argument (`expect(payload).not.toHaveProperty('overlayIdentifier')`, `.not.toHaveProperty('variableName')`), which is the ghost-is-gone evidence SC-004 asks for — a different instrument than the e2e cases, not a weaker substitute for them. Both are needed; neither alone closes SC-004 as written.
